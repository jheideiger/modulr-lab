who I am

from synthesizer to modular.

My name is Jeremy Heideiger. A musician passionate about sound design, I discovered electronic instruments in the 2000s with the Elektron Analog Four and the Octatrack — the culture of hardware, sequencing and going DAWless became an essential part of how I make music.

Then came modular. In 2020, I took the plunge, module by module, learning on the fly through the ModWiggler community. One encounter shaped this path in particular: Julia Bondar, whose work at Endorphin.es showed me a Eurorack can be far more than a collection of "blips and bloops" — a real instrument for composition.

Today my practice revolves around a Make Noise Shared System Plus and an Endorphin.es Shuttle System. But what matters most isn't the gear — it's the path taken to get there, and the fact that there's no real finish line.

sound as material

sound as material.

My relationship to modular is above all a relationship to sound design — in the spirit of what Radiohead did with synthesis: complementing traditional instruments rather than replacing them.

A patch isn't just a series of connections, it's a system that evolves: a modulation shifts a timbre, chance becomes an idea. And there's the gesture — turning a knob, moving a cable. That physical relationship to sound is fundamental to me: you don't draw the music, you handle it.

why modulr lab

why modulr lab?

modulr lab is the natural continuation of this journey. After a long time learning by reading, watching, exchanging with others and, above all, experimenting, I now want to pass it on — this is my teaching side.

I don't want to present modular as a world reserved for specialists. At its core, it's not a collection of machines: it's a way of making music, a laboratory where you learn by doing.
