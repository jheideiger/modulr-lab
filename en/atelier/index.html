→ workshop

workshop.

a few patches to try once the four lessons are done — to keep experimenting on your own.

→ why

practice before theory.

The course gave you the basics: signal, harmonics, fundamental modules. But the best way to make those ideas stick is to handle them yourself, with no detailed instructions, exploring rather than following.

The five patches below are deliberately described in just a few lines — enough to get you started, not enough to hold your hand. Try them in VCV Rack (free, already used in lesson 4) or on a real Eurorack system if you have one.

→ patches

creating vibrato

Plug a slow LFO (4 to 6 Hz) into a VCO's pitch CV input. Turn the LFO's amplitude down to the minimum, then bring it up gradually until you get a subtle vibrato instead of a siren.

modules: VCO, LFO

making a filter sing

On a VCF with high resonance, send a slow triangle LFO into the cutoff CV input. The filter starts to oscillate almost like a second oscillator — try detuning it slightly from the main VCO.

modules: VCF, LFO

a simple tremolo

Plug an LFO into a VCA's CV input. Try different LFO waveforms (square vs. triangle) to compare an abrupt tremolo with a smooth one.

modules: VCA, LFO

a note that breathes

On your ADSR, push Attack and Release to their maximum values, with a medium Sustain. Trigger a long note: the sound should appear and disappear so slowly you can no longer tell exactly where it starts or ends.

modules: ADSR, VCA

random melodies

If your setup includes a Sample & Hold module and a white noise source: sample the noise at regular intervals (via a clock or a second square LFO) and send the output to a VCO's pitch CV. You get a generative melody that never repeats exactly.

modules: Sample & Hold, white noise, clock, VCO

→ what's next

Once you've explored these five patches, you don't really need recipes anymore. The rest is curiosity and cables — over to you.
