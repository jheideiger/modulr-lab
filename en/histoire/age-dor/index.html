→ history 3/5

1960s – 1970s

the golden
age.

modular leaves the laboratory and moves into studios, films, and living rooms.

→ text

Wendy Carlos and the general public.

In 1968, composer Wendy Carlos released Switched-On Bach, an album where works by Johann Sebastian Bach are performed entirely on a Moog Modular synthesizer. The record became an unexpected commercial hit, won three Grammy Awards, and for the first time let a wide audience — far beyond academic or experimental circles — hear the sound of modular synthesis.

This success changed the perception of the modular synthesizer: it was no longer just a research tool, but also an instrument capable of interpreting classical repertoire and winning over a mainstream audience.

ARP and the cable-free patch.

Founded by Alan R. Pearlman, ARP Instruments released the ARP 2500 in 1970, a high-end modular system distinguished by a slider-based matrix patching system rather than physical cables — a choice that made the patch more robust on stage, at the cost of some rigidity compared to free cable routing. The ARP 2500 was famously used for the iconic sound effects in Close Encounters of the Third Kind.

ARP would also develop more compact synthesizers like the Odyssey, kicking off a trend toward miniaturization that would intensify in the following decades.

the Buchla 200 and the avant-garde.

Don Buchla continued his singular vision with the Buchla 200, an evolution of his early systems, adopted by composers close to experimental and electroacoustic music. Morton Subotnick used it for Silver Apples of the Moon (1967), a work that became a landmark of avant-garde electronic music, released on a classical music label — a sign that modular was starting to find its place across several musical worlds at once.

This decade also saw the rise of groups like Tangerine Dream and, a little later, Kraftwerk, who folded modular and electronics into popular music turned toward the future. But these systems remained expensive and bulky, reserved for professional studios and established artists — a constraint that would soon trigger a brutal reversal.
