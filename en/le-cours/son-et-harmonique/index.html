sound, harmonics
and timbre.

where the timbre of things comes from — and how a synthesizer creates it, then transforms it.

video
text

same note, two sounds.

In the previous lesson, we saw that a signal's frequency shapes the pitch we perceive. But frequency alone isn't enough to fully describe a sound.

At the start of the video, two sounds play the same note: a very simple sound, then a rich one. Listen to them before reading on. They have the same pitch, and yet they don't sound the same. So what changes?

fundamental and harmonics.

A sound we perceive as a single note can actually contain several frequencies. The main one is called the fundamental frequency: it's the one that notably sets the pitch we perceive.

Other frequencies can be added to it, at multiples of the fundamental: these are the harmonics. For a fundamental at 220 Hz, they sit at 440 Hz, 660 Hz, 880 Hz, and so on.

These extra frequencies change the shape of the signal, and therefore the way we perceive the sound. That's one of the reasons two sounds can share the same pitch while having different timbres.

waveforms.

In a synthesizer, this makeup depends notably on the shape of the signal produced by the oscillator. Sine, triangle, square, sawtooth: at the same pitch, the timbre changes, because each waveform has a different harmonic makeup.

The sine contains only the fundamental: it's the barest sound there is. The triangle adds weak odd harmonics. The square also contains odd harmonics, but stronger ones, which gives it its hollow timbre. The sawtooth contains all of them: it's the richest.

the filter.

We can also change this makeup after the signal has been produced — with a filter, for example. As the high frequencies of a rich sound are gradually reduced, you hear it get darker and darker.

The note being played hasn't changed. We've only altered some of the frequencies present in the signal.

two ways of looking at a sound.

We now have a second way of looking at a sound. We can watch how it evolves over time, but we can also look at the different frequencies it's made of. The fundamental tells us about pitch, the harmonics contribute to timbre, and in sound synthesis, we can act on this makeup to transform the sound.
