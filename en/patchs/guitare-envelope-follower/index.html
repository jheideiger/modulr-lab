a guitar in a modular synth · patch note 4 of 4

The guitar takes control: MATHS as an envelope follower

The guitar no longer goes through the machine, it drives it: the harder you play, the more the DPO's sound opens up. Fourth of the four patches in the video “J'ai branché ma guitare dans un synthé modulaire” (in French), to watch on the main page.

what you'll get.

A synth sound that breathes with your hands, without the guitar being heard.

→ listening

what to listen for.

  1. The level, first of all. Until the CV Bus LEDs show the right level, everything after it sounds weak and characterless. It isn't the patch that failed, it's the input.
  2. Fall. This is the setting that makes or breaks the patch musically: too short, the sound trembles on every attack; too long, it stays open between chords.
  3. The DPO, and only the DPO. The guitar isn't in the mix: you should hear the machine breathing with your hands. If the sound stays closed, the input level is too low and MATHS never rises high enough.
→ variations

three variations.

  1. MATHS's EOR output goes to 10 V on each peak: send it to STRIKE on Optomix channel 2, or to a sequencer, to trigger an event on every attack.
  2. Send the MATHS output to the DPO's Fold CV input rather than to the Optomix: playing hard folds the waveform instead of opening the sound.
  3. Bring a little guitar back into the mix, very low: the machine becomes the instrument's shadow.
→ your turn
