Guitar and Morphagene: breaking a chord into grains

A chord recorded into the Morphagene, cut into tiny grains and played back. By the end, nobody hears a guitar anymore. Third of the four patches in the video “J'ai branché ma guitare dans un synthé modulaire” (in French), to watch on the main page.

what you'll get.

A single two-second chord that becomes raw material: stretched, repeated, reversed.

→ listening

what to listen for.

  1. The level, first of all. Until the CV Bus LEDs show the right level, everything after it sounds weak and characterless. It isn't the patch that failed, it's the input.
  2. Gene-Size, turning clockwise. The recording shrinks into shorter and shorter fragments. Listen for the moment the chord's pitch stops being recognizable: that's where the guitar becomes raw material.
  3. Morph. The grains shift, stack up, overlap. A single chord starts to sound like several.
→ variations

three variations.

  1. Vari-Speed left of noon: playback runs backwards, and faster the further you turn.
  2. Slide driven by a slow modulation, for example MATHS cycling: the playback point wanders through the chord on its own.
  3. Record over it with S.O.S. around noon: the new chord blends with the old one instead of replacing it.
