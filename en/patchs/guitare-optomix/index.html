a guitar in a modular synth · patch note 1 of 4

Guitar and low pass gate: the struck chord

A sustained chord sent into the Optomix, and a clock that strikes it: the guitar sounds like a struck object. First of the four patches in the video “J'ai branché ma guitare dans un synthé modulaire” (in French), to watch on the main page.

result

what you'll get.

A sustained chord that turns into a series of strikes. On each hit, the sound opens up bright, then darkens as it fades.

level beginner

time 20 min

listening

what to listen for.

  1. The level, first of all. Until the CV Bus LEDs show the right level, everything after it sounds weak and characterless. It isn't the patch that failed, it's the input.
  2. Each strike. At the moment of the hit, the sound is bright; as it fades, it loses its highs along with its volume. That's what makes it sound “struck”: real objects sound like that.
  3. DAMP. At minimum, the strike is short and muffled; at maximum, it rings for a long time. Somewhere in between is the strike closest to wood.
variations

three variations.

  1. Replace the clock with the Pressure Points plates: the common gate into STRIKE, and you strike by hand. It's the “bongo” patch from the Optomix manual, which adds a channel gate into DAMP to mute the sound.
  2. Turn Fall while the chord sustains: the strikes speed up or slow down, without touching the guitar.
  3. Slowly raise CONTROL: the low pass gate no longer closes completely, and the strikes sit on a bed of sustained chord.
your turn

your turn to patch.

Set DAMP and Fall so that each strike fades out exactly as the next one arrives, with no silence and no overlap.
