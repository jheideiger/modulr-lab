patch note 7 · the Krell patch

The Krell patch: when the modular plays itself.

An envelope loops. At the end of each cycle it steps a sequence forward, and a random draw sets the length of the next note. Nobody touches the rack, and the patch plays. Here is how to build it on the Shared System Plus.

video
result

what you’ll get.

A sine wave playing a minor pentatonic scale, note after note, with no clock and no sequence written in advance. Every note has its own pitch, length and attack, and the Erbe-Verb’s reverb grows and shrinks as chance decides. The patch runs for as long as you let it.

level intermediate

time 45 min

modules MATHS · DPO · Optomix · René · Wogglebug · Erbe-Verb · CV Bus

listen

what to listen for.

  1. An envelope that loops. Patch cables 1 and 2, and the Optomix Sum Out straight into the CV Bus. With Cycle on, the same note comes back at a steady rate: a clock, nothing more. Listen to the Optomix: it is a low pass gate, opening volume and brightness together. The note starts bright and dies away as it softens.
  2. A new note every cycle. Add cables 3 and 4. At the end of each cycle, the EOC steps René forward: the pitch changes on every note, but all notes still last the same time.
  3. The random draw. Add cables 5, 6 and 7, channel 2 attenuverter at noon, then turn it up slowly: the lengths start to vary. The loop is closed. The envelope finishes its cycle, René steps, the Wogglebug draws, and the draw sets the length of the next note. A positive voltage on Fall lengthens the fall, a negative one shortens it.
  4. Attack and space. Add cables 8 and 9: some notes hit sharply, others rise slowly, and the rhythm moves a little more, since the rise is part of the cycle too. Then cables 10 to 13: the reverb, its tail following WOGGLE with a delay.
variations

three variations.

  1. A second voice. MATHS, channel 1 on Cycle, Unity to Optomix channel 2 Control; DPO, VCO B Sine output to channel 2 Signal In. Tune VCO B a fifth above the C, and give channel 1 a longer Rise and Fall than channel 4: the two voices drift apart and never lock. Sum Out already mixes both channels.
  2. Keeping fragments. Put the Morphagene after the Erbe-Verb: L and R outputs to its inputs, its outputs to the CV Bus. Press REC for a few notes, then again to stop: the Morphagene loops that fragment while the patch keeps going. S.O.S. around noon blends the patch and the fragment; fully clockwise, you only hear the fragment.
  3. Striking the notes. With a stackable cable or a multiple, also send René’s X-Gate to Optomix channel 1 STRIKE: every note gets struck, drier and louder. On René’s GATE page, turn off the gate on a few steps. Those notes are no longer struck, and the Wogglebug does not draw: they keep the length of the previous note.
your turn

your turn to listen.

Chance in reverse. Channel 2 attenuverter around 2 o’clock: listen for a minute and count the notes that last more than two seconds. Turn it to around 10 o’clock, below noon, and count again. Does the rhythm change character? Why do the very long notes almost all disappear?
